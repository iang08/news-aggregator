## Writing

Headline: v2 changes what gets picked. Between-arm overlap (J 0.39) is well below either arm's rep-vs-rep overlap (v1 0.71, v2 0.79). v2 is also the more repeatable arm, and it makes fewer picks (7.9 vs 11.7 per run, following its "never pad" rule).

Boilerplate. v1 uses "directly relevant" 4 times and a "directly <verb>" template in several more why-lines, e.g. "directly relevant to Kyberna's guardrails" and "Coordinated US-Japan jawboning on yen undervaluation directly moves Javan Imports' landed costs". v2 uses "directly relevant" 0 times but has its own templates. "bears directly on how Ian's autonomous researcher..." appears in several picks, and "Ian" is named 21 times against v1's 4. v2's top-level summary is formulaic: all 3 P0926new reps open "The most useful item today is...". The P0925 and P0926 reps are near-identical sentences: "The yen jumped as much as 1.2% to ¥156.94 per dollar after ... raising the dollar cost of auction buys for Javan and JCF." v1's top summaries are topic lists, which v2's prompt explicitly forbids: "Agent security and state power dominate: OpenAI agents' breach trail..., the Anthropic blacklist..., small open decision models..., while Japan pivots away from reflation under yen and bond pressure."

Naming the venture. v2 names a project or interest in 72% of why-lines against v1's 35% (my regex; the replay tool's own regex gives 75% vs 40%). v2 also gets the mapping right more often. v1 still routes things to "Founder's Play", a project v2's profile dropped, e.g. "directly relevant to Founder's Play-style tech-art work" for Willison's Kakapo tool. v1 also files the reward-hacking research-agent paper under Kyberna, where v2 correctly says "Ian's autonomous researcher". v2 adds a fitness pick v1 never makes: a PubMed bicarbonate-plus-carbohydrate trial with "a practical fueling tactic for long endurance sessions".

Currency direction. v2 gives the direction and its effect in all 10 of its currency picks, and gets it right: "A stronger yen raises the dollar cost of auction purchases for Javan and JCF". v1 mentions the yen in 6 picks. Some are right ("A firmer yen directly raises Javan's auction buy costs"), but others give no direction ("directly moves JDM import costs, so watch for intervention").

One story per pick. The automated near-duplicate title check found no pairs in either arm, but reading the picks shows one v2 lapse. Geopolitics. v1 still picks world/geopolitics (9% of picks, e.g. "Trump rejects Iranian peace proposal" and the Kadena missile deployment), justified as "crisis-venture scanning". v2 has 0% world picks.

Whether that is the right call for Ian is a judgment for the blind read. No other thinker blog surfaced in these pools, so this A/B does not really test v2's once-a-day essay rule.

## Verdict

## Conclusion

**v2 should replace v1 now.** It scored higher on all three pools, and in each pool every v2 rep scored above every v1 rep.

| pool | v1 mean (reps) | v2 mean (reps) | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0925 | 1.49 (1.42-1.56) | 1.74 (1.70-1.78) | +0.25 |
| P0926 | 1.28 (1.24-1.33) | 1.63 (1.56-1.67) | +0.35 |
| P0926new | 1.67 (1.58-1.73) | 1.95 (1.93-1.96) | +0.28 |

**The gain is precision, not more good items.** One run on each pool gives about 12.6 picks judged ≥2 for both arms. v2 gets there with 7.9 picks per run where v1 needs 11.7. Compared with v1, v2:
- drops world and geopolitics picks from 9% to 0%;
- names the venture in about 75% of why-lines, against 40%;
- gives the yen direction correctly in every currency pick;
- repeats itself more across reps (within-arm J 0.79 vs 0.71).

Overlap between the two arms is only 0.39, so v2 picks different stories, not the same ones reworded.

**Noise.**
- **Judges:** they disagree by 0.13-0.25 per item, which is close to the size of the gap. Each pool mean, though, averages 3 judges over 6-12 picks per run, and the gap points the same way in all 3 pools.
- **Pools:** there are really about 1.5 independent pools, not 3. P0925 and P0926 share the same Japan Times yen article.
- **Circularity:** the judges scored against the same 09-26 profile that v2 encodes. That shows v2 follows Ian's new preferences. It does not show those preferences are right.
- **Fewer picks score better:** the per-pick mean rewards a shorter list. On P0926, v2 found about 2.6 good items against v1's 3.6.
- **Essay rule not tested:** no thinker essay turned up in these pools. v2 left out the 蛭川 lecture, and the two judges who mentioned it gave it a 1.

**Fixes to make at cutover, in order:**
1. Ban the fixed opener "The most useful item today is..." and the stock phrase "bears directly on".
2. Make one pick per story strict. On P0926, v2 carried two yen picks, and its own why-line admits they overlap.
3. Rerun on 2-3 new pools that don't share articles, and check whether the P0926 recall dip happens again.

Verdict: adopt v2, confidence=medium
